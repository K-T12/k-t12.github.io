The Inner Planets

Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars

Rocky worlds, close to the Sun, small enough to hold onto only thin atmospheres — or none at all.

The Outer Planets

Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune

Gas and ice giants, vast enough that Jupiter alone could contain every other planet combined.

Landmark Missions

Pillars of Creation, Eagle Nebula — Credit: NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI

Voyager 1 & 2

Launched in 1977, both probes have now left the heliosphere and are sending data back from interstellar space.

James Webb Space Telescope

Observing in infrared from a stable orbit beyond the Moon, revealing galaxies as they appeared over 13 billion years ago.

Artemis Program

NASA's effort to return humans to the lunar surface and establish a long-term presence as a stepping stone to Mars.

Event Horizon Telescope

A network of ground-based observatories that produced the first direct image of a black hole's shadow in 2019.

Phenomena Worth Watching For

Black holes — regions where gravity is so strong that not even light can escape

Supernovae — the explosive death of a massive star, briefly outshining entire galaxies

Nebulae — vast clouds of gas and dust where new stars are born

Exoplanets — worlds orbiting other stars, over 5,000 confirmed so far
